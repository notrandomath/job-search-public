# Leveling Rubric

Purpose: prevent matching a resume to roles above (or far below) the person's demonstrated experience. Bands are intentionally described by concrete signals, not vibes. When in doubt, pick the lower band and say so explicitly.

Bands used generically as L3 (entry/early career) / L4 (mid) / L5 (senior) — map to whatever ladder the user's target companies actually use (levels differ: "L3" at one company ≈ "SDE II" at another). Treat these as archetypes, not universal numbers.

## Software Engineering (SWE) track

**L3 / entry-early career**
- 0-2 years full-time experience, or strong internship(s) + degree, no full-time experience yet
- Delivered features/projects with guidance; limited unsupervised system-design ownership
- 1-2 internships, or up to ~1 year full-time, counts here even with strong project portfolio

**L4 / mid**
- ~2-5 years full-time experience
- Owns a component or service end-to-end with minimal guidance; some cross-team coordination
- Internship experience alone, however impressive, does not reach this band without full-time tenure

**L5 / senior**
- 5+ years full-time, track record of technical leadership, design decisions with org-wide impact, mentoring

## ML Engineer / Applied Scientist track

Same YOE bands as SWE, but weight production ML ownership (shipped models, pipelines at scale) over research novelty. A strong Kaggle/personal project record does not by itself justify L4 — it substitutes partially for missing production experience but should still land at L3 unless paired with real deployment ownership.

## Counting school, research and projects toward "years of experience"

A stated years requirement is usually soft, and academic work often counts. Treat the person's effective experience as full-time years plus internships and co-ops (a summer is a fraction of a year), plus research, a thesis, or substantial project work when the posting allows it. Read the sentence around the requirement for qualifiers such as "or equivalent experience", "including internships", "research", or an advanced degree that substitutes for years. When one is present, a posting asking for a year or two (even three) more than the person's full-time years is a **stretch worth applying to**, not a rejection: say which part of their record covers it. A stated number is soft even when concrete: whether it filters the application depends on the recruiter, so treat it as odds, not a wall. What is hard is what the system or the law enforces: work authorization or a sponsorship refusal, citizenship, a clearance, and knockout questions such as a required graduation window, student status, or a degree they lack. Never offer a role that fails a hard requirement. State what you could not tell and suggest the person ask the recruiter.

## Research Scientist track

Leveling here hinges more on publication record and research independence than YOE:

**Entry-level RS / RS I**
- MS or early PhD, or strong research internship(s), 0-1 first-author publications at a recognized venue, or workshop-paper-only record
- Research was scoped/directed substantially by an advisor or mentor

**Mid RS / RS II**
- PhD (often required) with 2+ first-author publications at recognized venues, demonstrated ability to independently scope a research direction

**Senior RS**
- Established independent research agenda, multiple strong publications, often some mentoring/leadership track record

Important: a few weeks of research with no publication should be tiered like a strong project, not like a research role — do not let "did research" alone imply RS-track fit.

## Cross-cutting rules
- A postdoc or PhD alone does not automatically justify a senior band — check independence and publication signals specifically.
- If a resume shows internship + no full-time experience, default to entry-level band even if the internship was at a prestigious company.
- Always extract the posting's *own* stated requirement (YOE, degree, "new grad" language) and treat a clear mismatch as a hard filter, not a soft signal.
- In domain and keyword matching, heavily weight skills and tech stacks from industry internships and full-time positions over standalone class or side projects.

## Beyond engineering, and beyond L5

- **Other functions (data, product, design, finance, operations, research outside ML, and so on):** the engineering ladder above does not apply. Ask the candidate for their field's ladder or use the target employers' published levels, then calibrate with the same evidence tests: scope owned, independence, outcomes, and years of full-time tenure.
- **Staff and principal / management track (roughly 8+ years):** judge on scope across teams, technical or organizational leadership, and hiring or mentoring record. A manager and a staff IC at the same tenure are different tracks; ask which the candidate wants.
- **Career changer:** calibrate on transferable seniority from the previous field and state plainly what is new. Expect a lower band in the new domain than the old tenure suggests, and say what evidence (projects, certifications, an internal move) would raise it.
- **PhD or postdoc moving to industry:** map research years to a band by independence and impact (first-author work, shipped systems, grants or leadership of a project), not by years alone. Many industry research and applied-science ladders place a fresh PhD above entry level; verify against the target employer's own ladder.
- **Returning after a gap:** judge by prior experience and recency of skills; do not penalize the gap itself.
- **Self-employed or founder experience:** translate scope (team size, revenue, systems shipped) into employer terms; do not treat it as zero tenure.

## Interview history is evidence of level, not of what the resume passes cold

Weight each past interview by how it came about. Cold applications and recruiter outreach show what a screener let through, so they set the title pattern and band. Referral interviews show the level people were willing to put the person forward for, but a referral can carry a resume past a screen it would not clear alone, so do not treat referral interviews as proof of what the resume passes cold, and do not copy the referrers' employers into the target list. Interviews for roles the person was not targeting show what they can get, not what they want. With fewer than about three cold or recruiter data points, say the calibration rests mostly on the resume and this rubric.
