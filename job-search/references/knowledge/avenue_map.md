# Avenue Map: The Coverage Checklist

The counselor's job is to not leave out a relevant avenue. Before writing advice or searching, walk this entire map and mark each avenue **relevant / not relevant (reason) / not applicable**. Anything marked relevant must appear in the deliverables with concrete names, links, and next steps. Anything marked not relevant is silently dropped; the user does not need to read the whole map.

Names below are illustrative seeds, not authoritative or current. Verify before citing, and add the ones that fit this person's field. The categories are the point.

## A. Direct employer channels

| Avenue | Typical examples | Relevant when |
|---|---|---|
| Mega-cap and large tech | big public tech firms (early-career programs and open reqs) | most tech profiles |
| Frontier AI labs and research orgs | research and engineering roles at AI labs; residencies and fellowships for early career | ML / AI goal |
| Late-stage private companies | unicorns and decacorns with early-career programs | wants scale plus upside |
| Hardware, semiconductor, systems vendors | accelerator, compiler, driver, networking teams | systems / ML infra spike |
| Finance and quant | quant research / trading / fintech engineering | comp-driven, math or systems strength |
| Non-tech-first large employers | healthcare, automotive, robotics, defense, energy, bio | domain pivot or location bound |
| Federal civilian agencies and their digital teams | agency digital-service and modernization teams, central digital-service programs | public-interest product or web work, stability; US work authorization usually required |
| Civic-tech organizations and fellowships | civic-tech nonprofits, tech-in-government fellowships | public-interest work, product or web skills, mission-driven teams |
| Government contractors | firms that build and run agency systems (including small agile contractors) | government-facing work without a federal hire process; clearance and citizenship often required |
| State and local government | state, city and county technology and web teams | local roles, web or product work, steadier hours |
| National labs, FFRDCs, public research | national labs, research agencies | research flavour, stability, clearance-eligible |
| Non-profits and NGOs | mission-driven organizations with engineering teams | mission over pay, smaller teams |
| Academia-adjacent | research engineer in a university lab, PhD route as an alternative | research goal, publication record |

## B. Startup and venture avenues (do not omit for any startup-leaning goal)

| Avenue | Examples | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| VC portfolio job boards | major firm portfolio boards (major firms in the user's own region; for the US this includes names like a16z, Sequoia, Accel, Index, Greylock) | filter by domain tags and stage; many firms host a talent board |
| Accelerator batches | YC (Work at a Startup), Techstars, Entrepreneur First, South Park Commons | filter latest two batches by domain |
| Curated startup boards | Wellfound, Otta/Welcome to the Jungle, Work at a Startup, "AI startup jobs" style boards | search by niche, not "software engineer" |
| Seed to Series B company lists | funding announcements, fund "portfolio" pages, newsletters | recently funded means hiring but no brand-name pipeline |
| Community hiring threads | monthly "Who is hiring" threads, Discord and Slack job channels, niche mailing lists | small teams that never post on big boards |
| Founder / operator network | cold email to founders, "founding engineer" roles | high variance, high scope |

## C. Stage-specific channels (use the rows that match the person's situation)

| Avenue | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Early career:** university recruiting and cohort reqs | the dominant channel for students; see [`hiring-channels.md`](hiring-channels.md) |
| Campus career fairs and school job boards | check the user's own university; often has exclusive postings |
| Intern conversion and return offers | an active pipeline excludes the company |
| Fellowships and residencies | fixed-term research or engineering programs with cohort dates |
| Community GitHub trackers | daily-updated boards, mostly early-career and internship; see [`github_job_boards.md`](../sources/github_job_boards.md) |
| **Experienced:** recruiters and executive search | retained and contingency recruiters in the field; keep a short list and brief them precisely |
| **Experienced:** former managers and colleagues | the highest-conversion channel; ask for introductions, not jobs |
| **Experienced:** internal mobility and counter-offers | for employed candidates; plan the sequence and the notice period |
| **Experienced:** industry job boards and professional societies | field-specific boards, member directories, association job boards |
| **Returning after a gap:** returnship and re-entry programs | structured programs for returners; also contract and part-time bridges |
| **Career changer:** adjacent roles, rotational programs, internal transfers | build evidence in the new field while employed or via projects, certifications and writing |
| **PhD / postdoc:** industry research labs vs applied roles vs academic positions | map defense date to start windows; note research-scientist vs applied-scientist ladders |

## D. Relationship-driven avenues

| Avenue | Concrete forms |
|---|---|
| Warm contacts you already have | people who referred or interviewed you before, former managers, colleagues now at the targets; who to ask for intros and what to say, built from the person's own interview history | the person shared an interview history or referrals |
| Alumni, colleagues, and lab network | former colleagues and managers, advisors and labs, school or program alumni now at targets; search recipes built from the person's real history |
| Referrals | who can refer, how to ask, what to attach |
| Conferences and workshops | specific venues in the field with career events, sponsor lists, submission deadlines |
| Open-source contribution | projects in the user's stack whose maintainers hire |
| Writing and demos | posts, benchmarks, reproductions that make a profile findable |
| Recruiter and talent platforms | skills-based matching sites, LinkedIn recruiter settings, inbound optimisation |
| Professional societies and meetups | local chapter events in the user's metro |

## E. Alternative routes (raise only when they fit the goal)

- Contract, consulting, or freelance work as a bridge.
- Further study (PhD, research master's) when the goal is research and offers are thin.
- Remote-first and international employers when location-bound or visa-limited.
- Internal transfers or return-offer conversion for someone already employed.
- Starting something, when the goal answer points that way.

| Work the person wants | Channels and sectors that carry it most often |
|---|---|

| Full-stack product | startups and growth-stage companies, civic-tech and agency digital teams, agencies and consultancies, mid-size product companies |
| Data and analytics | finance, health, government statistical agencies, product companies, research orgs |
| ML and applied research | AI labs, big tech research, national labs, universities, startups with ML cores |

## F. Fit map: goal to avenues

| Goal archetype | Must cover | Usually skip |
|---|---|---|
| Best-paying / brand | A (mega-cap, labs, late-stage, quant), C, D referrals | most of B beyond top-tier funds |
| Technical depth in one problem | A labs / hardware, D conferences and open source, B niche boards | broad general-SWE tracks |
| Startup upside | all of B, D founder network, C trackers | mega-cap pipelines except as fallback |
| Pivot | A non-tech-first, B niche boards, C career-changer rows, D writing and demos, E study | senior-only channels |
| Senior IC or management | A, B (founding and leadership roles), C experienced rows, D referrals | campus pipelines, entry-level trackers |
| Returning after a gap | C returner rows, A employers with returnships, B small teams, E contract bridges | campus pipelines |
| Employed and discreet | D referrals via trusted contacts, C recruiters, A open reqs | public networking posts, anything that tips off the current employer |
| Non-tech or non-engineering field | the field's own boards and societies (C), A employers in that sector, D | tech-specific trackers and ATS sweeps |
| Any offer by a date | C, A broad sweep, B boards, D referrals | long-lead research programs |
| Government, lab, or non-profit | the sub-route that matches the work they want (see A and E2), D warm contacts, C boards for public service | routes that need a clearance or citizenship the person lacks |
| Visa / location bound | filter A and B by sponsorship and location first, then E remote | companies that never sponsor |

## Resume-derived avenue generator

The fixed map above is the same for everyone. This step makes the avenues specific to the person: go through each project, paper, tool, employer, lab, and course on the resume and ask what a person with that exact artifact could do that others could not. Produce candidates, then keep the **5 most promising** for the advice document, ranked alongside the general avenues.

For each resume item, consider:

| Move | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Contribute upstream | Did they modify or build on an open-source project? A PR, write-up, or issue reply puts them in front of its maintainers and users, who often hire. |
| Contact the authors | Whose work does their paper or project extend or beat? A specific follow-up note to those authors, who work at known teams, is stronger than a cold application. |
| Adjacent companies | Which companies build with the exact stack, domain, or method on the resume (voice, agents, simulation, robotics, dev tools, a scientific field)? These are rarely on new-grad trackers. |
| Programs and pipelines | Which fellowships, visiting-researcher, student-researcher, or affiliate programs feed teams that use this skill? |
| Build one small thing | What could they build or evaluate for a target's public product, API, or paper in a weekend that shows the skill (a benchmark, a plugin, a reproduction)? |
| Write it up | Is there a result or lesson worth a short public post that a hiring engineer would find? |
| Events and communities | Where do people who care about this topic gather (workshops, meetups, hackathons with sponsors, paper groups)? |
| Alumni of the item | Who previously worked on the same lab, course, or team, and where are they now? |
| Insurance routes | If openings are thin, which route keeps momentum (research engineer in an academic lab, a graduate program application with a near deadline, a contract or bridge role)? |

Rules:
- Tie each kept avenue to the specific resume item that makes it credible, in the avenue's own text.
- Prefer avenues that need an hour or a weekend over ones that need months.
- Name real programs and organizations only when you have checked they exist and are current; otherwise describe the type and how to search for it.
- Never invent people, papers, or deadlines.

## G. Self-check before finishing

Ask: if a domain expert read this, which avenue would they say is missing? Then check specifically: VC portfolios and startup boards (B), research and residency programs (A, C), the person's own network: colleagues, school, lab (D), and sponsorship constraints (intake). If a category was excluded, the advice must say why in one line. Then check every avenue you wrote: if it only says "apply to these companies" or "search a job site for these titles", it is not an avenue; replace it with the channel behind it (who to message, which board or community, what to build or contribute) or delete it.

## H. Rough prestige buckets (user-anchored, contestable)

These buckets describe tech employers. For another field, ask for the person's own ladder of employers.

This is a loose bucketing scheme to help sort search results by the user's own stated target, not an objective ranking. Always let the user set or override the anchor company/tier at the start of a search.

## Suggested buckets
1. **Frontier / mega-cap (tech)**: large-cap tech companies and leading AI labs with substantial compensation and brand recognition (e.g. the largest public tech companies, best-known AI research labs)
2. **Well-funded scale-ups**: late-stage/well-known unicorns, high-growth companies with strong engineering reputations
3. **Funded startups (seed-Series C)**: smaller, higher variance in prestige/comp, often higher role scope for the level
4. **Early-stage / pre-seed-Series A**: highest variance, hardest to verify "prestige" — evaluate case by case (founding team background, funding, traction)
5. **Public mid/large-cap non-tech-first**: established companies with strong eng orgs but less "tech brand" cachet

## How to use
- Ask the user for their anchor (e.g. "match or exceed my current company") and which bucket that anchor falls in.
- Tag search results with a bucket + one-line justification (funding stage, headcount, public recognition) — don't just assert a tier without evidence.
- Treat this as directional sorting, not a hard filter, unless the user asks for a hard filter.
- If a company's tier is genuinely unclear or contested, say so rather than picking one confidently.
