# Trajectory Test: Is This Role a Step Forward?

Applies in **standard and deep** runs only. Lite mode skips it and does not offer stepping-stone roles at all (see `lite_mode.md`).

Run it on every contract, QA or test, support, IT, agency-placed, or otherwise adjacent role before offering it, and whenever a role's title sits below the person's demonstrated level. A role that fails stays out of the list.

## The questions

Answer each from the posting text and the employer, not from the title alone.

| # | Question | Forward | Dead end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What would the resume say afterward? | Real engineering in the person's target stack, with scope and outcomes they can write down | Ticket-closing, manual testing, or maintenance with no ownership to point to |
| 2 | Does the employer give a path in? | Named employer with a record of converting contractors, or an internal mobility route | Staffing agency with no stated conversion, or an employer known for rolling contractors over |
| 3 | Employer signal | Recognizable engineering brand, or a strong team, so the line itself carries weight | Unknown agency client, or no way to tell who the client is |
| 4 | Duration and renewal | Defined term (about 6 to 12 months) with a stated extension or conversion path | Open-ended, repeated short renewals, or "until project ends" |
| 5 | Does it shorten the path to the person's goal? | Keeps them in their field, builds the exact skills the goal needs, or fills a gap | Moves them away from the goal's skills, or competes for the time they would spend applying to better roles |
| 6 | Can they keep searching? | Time, schedule and no non-compete to keep interviewing | Hours or restrictions that would stall the search |

## Reading the result

- **Mostly forward:** offer it, with a one-line reason.
- **Mixed:** offer it only in the stepping-stone table, state the trade-off in one line, and say what would make it worth taking.
- **Mostly dead end:** drop it, and say in "Not listed and why" that it was dropped for trajectory, not for availability.

## Adjust for the person's state

- **Already held a software-engineering role:** be stricter. QA, support and agency contract work after SWE experience usually reads as a step back. Offer such roles only when the person asked, or when question 1 and 5 are clearly forward.
- **Long gap, career changer, or returning to work:** be more permissive. A contract with real engineering work can be the fastest way to get a current line on the resume. Say that explicitly.
- **New graduate or very early career:** contract and apprenticeship roles are often a legitimate way in; apply the test, and prefer those with a conversion path.
- **Currently employed:** a contract rarely beats staying put; apply the test against what they already have.
- **The person explicitly wants it** (for example, wants contract for flexibility or income): honor that, still state any trade-off once, and do not argue.

## Writing it up

For each stepping-stone role shown, add one line: what the resume gains, the conversion or exit route if any, and the main risk. Never present one as the main recommendation unless the person asked for it.
